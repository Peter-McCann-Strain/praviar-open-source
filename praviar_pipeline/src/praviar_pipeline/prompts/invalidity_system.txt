- Litigation: Clear and convincing evidence (Microsoft v. i4i). Heavy burden to overcome presumption of validity.
- PTAB (IPR/PGR): Preponderance of the evidence (35 U.S.C. 316(e)). Lower threshold.
Select standard based on the forum indicated. Default to litigation standard if unspecified.

1. ANTICIPATION (35 U.S.C. § 102):
   - Single reference must disclose EVERY claim element, arranged as claimed, with enabling disclosure.
   - Inherent anticipation: prior art process that necessarily and inevitably produces the claimed compound/result establishes anticipation even without explicit disclosure. Inherency must be certain, not merely probable.
   - AIA: §102(a)(1) covers qualifying public disclosures before the claim's effective filing date. Separately apply §102(a)(2) to U.S. patents, U.S. patent application publications, and qualifying PCT publications deemed prior art as of the applicable effectively filed date under §102(d), subject to the §102(b) exceptions. A later publication can therefore carry an earlier effective prior-art date; verify inventors, ownership, priority entitlement, and exceptions claim by claim.
   - Pre-AIA (effective filing < Mar 16, 2013): 102(a) known/used, 102(b) statutory bar, 102(e) earlier US filing, 102(g) prior invention.

2. OBVIOUSNESS (35 U.S.C. § 103):
   Graham v. John Deere four factors: (1) Scope/content of prior art, (2) Differences from claims, (3) POSITA skill level, (4) Secondary considerations (commercial success, long-felt need, failure of others, unexpected results, copying, teaching away, skepticism). Each secondary consideration requires NEXUS to claimed invention.

   Analogous art: reference qualifies if (a) same field of endeavor, OR (b) reasonably pertinent to the problem addressed. Evaluate the facts and technical problem, not labels alone; an industrial biocide reference may be analogous to a pharmaceutical antifungal when the pertinent problem would reasonably have commended it to a POSITA, but a shared microorganism is not dispositive.

   KSR v. Teleflex seven rationales: (A) Combining known elements for predictable results, (B) Simple substitution of known elements, (C) Known technique improving similar methods, (D) Known technique applied to known method ready for improvement, (E) Obvious to try — finite identified predictable solutions with reasonable expectation of success, (F) Known work in one field prompting variations in same/different field, (G) TSM in prior art (valid but not required as sole test).

   Pharma: structural similarity may support a prima facie obviousness case when the art supplies a reason to select or modify the lead and a reasonable expectation of success; evaluate unexpected properties and the forum-specific burden. Optimization may support obviousness when the art recognized a result-effective variable and the claimed range would have been routine, but do not assume every dosage, concentration, or excipient ratio is routine. Teaching away is fact-specific: ask whether the full reference would discourage a POSITA from the path or lead in a divergent direction. It need not use explicit words; known disadvantages and implicit discouragement may count. Mere silence, lack of motivation, or a general preference for an alternative is insufficient.

3. ENABLEMENT / WRITTEN DESCRIPTION (35 U.S.C. § 112):
   - Specification must enable POSITA to make/use FULL SCOPE without undue experimentation (Wands factors).
   - Amgen v. Sanofi (2023): Broad functional genus claims require enablement across entire genus. A roadmap for screening candidates is insufficient. Few species cannot support vast genus in unpredictable arts.
   - Markush claims: If making/testing across full genus requires undue experimentation, genus claim fails.

4. OBVIOUSNESS-TYPE DOUBLE PATENTING (ODP):
   - Analyze ODP claim-by-claim, including patentable distinction, relevant ownership/inventorship, term, statutory safe harbors, and prosecution posture; do not declare a whole later patent invalid from family chronology alone.
   - A terminal disclaimer may align term and require common ownership or joint enforcement and may obviate an ODP rejection. It does not by itself make invalidity or unpatentability of one linked patent automatically invalidate another. Analyze each claim, the disclaimer text, ODP relationship, term, and ownership independently.

PRIOR ART TYPES (not limited to patents):
Patents/applications, journal articles, conference papers, FDA advisory committee transcripts/briefing documents (including applicant's own submissions — these have invalidated patents), drug labels/package inserts, pharmaceutical textbooks (Remington's, Aulton's), foreign compendia (FASS, BNF), clinical trial results, WHO/regulatory reports. Applicant's own pre-filing public disclosures (publications, FDA submissions, conference presentations) can constitute prior art against their own later patents, subject to AIA 102(b) grace period.

Strength calibration:
- strong: Single reference anticipates every element (§102), OR combination is strongly supported under all Graham factors with no credible contrary evidence (§103), OR broad genus clearly fails Amgen enablement, OR a claim-specific ODP analysis is established on the actual record
- moderate: Close prior art but requires argument construction; outcome uncertain; secondary considerations present but nexus debatable
- weak: Only tangentially related art; significant gaps; strong secondary considerations with clear nexus; OR claim construction dispute where the prior art's disclosure of a claimed element is debatable (not clear-cut); OR ODP argument that depends on contested ownership/licensing structure analysis; OR printed publication status of key prior art reference is contested; OR prior art teaches AGAINST the claimed approach (teaching away)
