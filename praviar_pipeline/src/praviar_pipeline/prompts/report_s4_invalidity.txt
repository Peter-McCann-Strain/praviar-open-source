**A. Prior Art Invalidity Arguments**

Graham v. John Deere Co., 383 U.S. 1 (1966) factors:
1. Scope and content of the prior art
2. Differences between prior art and claims at issue
3. Level of ordinary skill in the art (state: "A POSITA is [description, e.g., PhD in medicinal chemistry with 5+ years pharma R&D experience]")
4. Objective indicia of nonobviousness — address ALL four categories where data available:
   - Unexpected results (with nexus to claimed invention)
   - Long-felt need (unresolved problem in the field)
   - Industry praise (awards, recognition)
   - Commercial success (with nexus — not just market size)

For each prior art reference from tool data:
- Full citation: Author(s), Title, Journal/Source, Year, DOI
- Publication date vs. priority date of the patent (confirm it qualifies as prior art)
- Whether it anticipates (35 U.S.C. 102) or renders obvious (35 U.S.C. 103)
- Which claim elements are disclosed by this reference

KSR Int'l Co. v. Teleflex Inc., 550 U.S. 398 (2007) obviousness rationales (apply when supported by data):
(A) Combining prior art elements with known methods for predictable results
(B) Simple substitution of known elements to obtain predictable results
(C) Use of known technique to improve similar devices/methods in the same way
(D) Applying a known technique to a known device/method ready for improvement to yield predictable results
(E) "Obvious to try" — choosing from a finite number of identified, predictable solutions with a reasonable expectation of success
(F) Known work in one field of endeavor prompting variations in the same or different field
(G) Teaching, suggestion, or motivation in the prior art to modify or combine references

**B. PTAB Proceedings**
For each PTAB proceeding (from `get_patent_details`):
- Case number (e.g., IPR2017-01712)
- Type: IPR (inter partes review), PGR (post-grant review), or CBM
- Petitioner
- Claims challenged / claims cancelled / claims survived
- Grounds by proceeding type. For IPR, 35 U.S.C. § 311(b) permits only §§ 102/103 grounds based on patents or printed publications. Do not attribute written-description or enablement grounds to an IPR. For PGR/other proceedings, state only the grounds the authoritative record actually shows.
- Status: instituted, final written decision, settled, terminated
- Outcome and implications for remaining claims

KEY SIGNALS:
- Claims found unpatentable or cancelled: identify exact claims, ground, FWD, Director review/rehearing, appeal, remand, certificate, and effective-cancellation posture. Do not call a claim presently cancelled or unenforceable until the authoritative current record supports that legal effect.
- Proceeding settled or terminated: report the event and surviving posture only. Do not infer validity, weakness, enforceability, or willingness to license without case-specific evidence.
- Institution or denial: state the articulated basis. Discretionary denial may be unrelated to merits, and even a merits-based institution decision is preliminary; neither establishes patent strength or final validity.

**C. Prosecution History Estoppel**
From narrowing amendments during prosecution:
- Specific narrowing amendment made
- Prior art the amendment was made to overcome
- Scope of estoppel under Festo Corp. v. Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo Kabushiki Co., 535 U.S. 722 (2002)
- Three Festo rebuttal pathways: (1) unforeseeable equivalent, (2) tangential purpose, (3) other reason
- Impact on Doctrine of Equivalents — which equivalents are now foreclosed

**D. Obviousness-Type Double Patenting (ODP) and Terminal Disclaimers**
- If same assignee has multiple related patents: check for ODP vulnerability
- Terminal disclaimer chains may align term and impose common-ownership or joint-enforcement conditions, but invalidity of one linked patent does not automatically invalidate every disclaimed child. Describe the actual disclaimer, ODP relationship, claims, term, ownership, and adjudication posture.
- Note which patents in the portfolio are linked by terminal disclaimers

**E. Enablement and Written Description (for biologics/genus claims)**
- Amgen Inc. v. Sanofi, 598 U.S. 594 (2023) — genus claims covering antibodies require enablement across the full scope
- Written description: adequate number of representative species disclosed?
- Enablement: undue experimentation required to practice the full scope? Apply Wands factors.
- This is particularly relevant for broad Markush claims in composition-of-matter patents
- For antibody patents: assess whether claims define antibodies by structure (CDR sequences, VH/VL regions) or by function (binding affinity, epitope). Functional claims are particularly vulnerable post-Amgen. Claims defined by CDR/VH/VL sequences are narrower and harder to invalidate but easier to design around.

**F. Doctrine of Equivalents Summary**
For each patent with DoE assessments from tool data:
- Which NOT_MET elements were assessed under DoE
- Function-Way-Result test results per element
- Confidence band: HIGH confidence (likely equivalent) / MODERATE (arguable) / LOW (speculative)
- Prosecution history estoppel limitations — which equivalents are blocked by narrowing amendments
- Overall DoE conclusion: does DoE convert a non-infringing result into infringement?
