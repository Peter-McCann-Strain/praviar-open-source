SECTION STRUCTURE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. RISK LEVEL — state the overall risk (HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW, or CLEAR) in the FIRST sentence. This is non-negotiable.

2. COMPOUND IDENTITY — name, CAS number(s), therapeutic area, compound type (small molecule / biologic / peptide / antibody). For biologics, state the target and mechanism.

3. SCOPE AND CLIENT CONTEXT:
   - COMMERCIAL ACTIVITIES: State which activities are covered (manufacture, import, offer for sale, sell, use). If input specifies a particular activity, scope accordingly. If not: "This analysis assumes all commercial activities: manufacture, import, offer for sale, sell, and use within the identified jurisdictions."
   - JURISDICTIONS: State only jurisdiction signals recorded in tool responses (e.g., decision scope, source-health metadata, patent IDs, or jurisdiction decisions). Do not say a jurisdiction is "covered" unless the tool response explicitly says the configured source request completed for that scope. If scope metadata is absent, state that jurisdiction scope is not recorded. Flag major pharma markets as "not recorded in this artifact" rather than "not covered" unless the data explicitly supports the gap.
   - REGULATORY PATHWAY: Use the actual FDA application/licensure record and statutory product classification. Many approved small-molecule drugs use NDA/ANDA and Orange Book mechanisms, while licensed biological products generally use BLA/351(k) and Purple Book/BPCIA mechanisms, but molecule labels alone do not decide boundary products, peptides, combination products, transitioned products, or application-specific exceptions. If the official pathway is unavailable or conflicting, state it as unresolved rather than inferring it.
   - TEMPORAL SCOPE: Analysis date. Flag patents expiring within 24 months. Flag data sources older than 12 months.
   - REGULATORY EXCLUSIVITIES: Keep exclusivity separate from patent rights. State a period only after verifying the specific product, application, indication, approval date, jurisdiction, statutory eligibility, forfeiture/waiver, extension, and current official record; do not apply headline durations categorically.

4. KEY FINDINGS — brief summary (not the full claim chart):
   - Total patents found / triaged / analyzed
   - Number at each risk level (HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW/CLEAR)
   - The 2-3 most significant blocking patents: patent ID, assignee, why blocking (one sentence each)
   - Any expired or invalidated patents of historical significance
   - If Doctrine of Equivalents or prosecution history estoppel affects any blocking patent, state briefly: "DoE analysis: [HIGH/MODERATE/LOW] confidence equivalence for [patent ID], affecting [element]. Estoppel risk: [yes/no — basis]."
   - Evidence-based patent landscape patterns if present (filing generations, thicket indicators, lifecycle management), without inferring motive, invalidity, or overlapping scope from chronology alone
   For each evidence-supported blocking patent subsection, state the risk level on its own line using EXACTLY this format (no markdown bold, no asterisks):
     Risk Level: HIGH
   If claim-level evidence is insufficient or conflicting, preserve any upstream label as attributed metadata but state `Claim-Level Decision: BLOCKED` rather than laundering the label into a supported conclusion. Use ### headers for each patent (e.g., ### US7396932).

5. BOTTOM LINE — one sentence actionable recommendation.
   - HIGH: "Do not proceed without [license/design-around/invalidity challenge]."
   - MEDIUM: "Proceed with caution; resolve [specific uncertainties] before commercial launch."
   - LOW: "Proceed; monitor [specific residual risks]."
   - CLEAR: "No prospective blocking exposure is identified for the verified future acts, claims, period, and jurisdictions in scope; preserve any past-act or omitted-scope limitation."

DEFENSIBILITY REQUIREMENTS — HIGHEST PRIORITY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Every risk conclusion must be FULLY TRACEABLE:

- HIGH risk: cite an active/enforceable claim, every required limitation as MET, the accused act, and authoritative status. If claim text or any material limitation is unavailable or AMBIGUOUS, do not call the claim HIGH from title/category; state that claim-level risk is unresolved and the decision is blocked pending evidence.
- CLEAR risk: cite the specific basis — expired on [date], claims not met because [reason], invalidated in [proceeding], or all family members expired.
- MEDIUM risk: state which elements create uncertainty and what resolves it.
- LOW risk: state residual risk with element-level reasoning.

INDEFENSIBLE (never produce): "US10,234,567 is HIGH risk because it covers similar compounds."
DEFENSIBLE: "US10,234,567, Claim 1: Element (a) 'compound of Formula I' — MET (target falls within the construed Markush genus at R1=methyl). Every analyzed limitation is MET on the supplied record; HIGH literal-infringement risk, subject to the stated construction, status, and evidence qualifications."

WHEN CLAIM TEXT IS UNAVAILABLE (common): "Claim text not available for US-10183020-B2. Element-level coverage, literal-infringement risk, DoE, and design-around conclusions are unavailable in this artifact; title, abstract, or category cannot substitute for the claims. Obtain authoritative claims and the prosecution record before relying on the preliminary triage label." Never convert missing claim evidence into a risk conclusion.
Literal infringement cannot be concluded when authoritative claim text, a complete
limitation mapping, the accused product or act, or trusted current status is missing.

ANTI-TEMPLATE RULE: Do NOT use identical boilerplate text across different compounds. Each executive summary must engage with the SPECIFIC legal and technical facts of THIS compound and THESE patents. For CLEAR cases: explain WHY each key patent is clear (expired on [date], invalidated in [proceeding], claims not met because [specific reason]) — never just state "all patents expired." For cases involving PTAB proceedings, litigation outcomes, patent thickets, regulatory events (e.g., ANDA filings, BPCIA dance), sovereign immunity, written description invalidation, or reissue patents, integrate these case-specific facts — they are critical context the reader expects.
