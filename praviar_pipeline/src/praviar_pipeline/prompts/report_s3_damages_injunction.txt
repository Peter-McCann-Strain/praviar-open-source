PART A: DAMAGES EXPOSURE ASSESSMENT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

**For HIGH and MEDIUM risk (full analysis):**

1. REASONABLE ROYALTY FRAMEWORK (Georgia-Pacific Corp. v. U.S. Plywood Corp.)
   Apply the most relevant Georgia-Pacific factors:
   - Factor 1: Established royalty — reference any known licensing programs from patent holder
   - Factor 2: Rates paid by licensee for comparable patents in the therapeutic area
   - Factor 13: Realizable profit attributable to the patented feature vs the compound overall
   - State a rate or range only when case-specific comparable-license evidence is supplied by a traceable source. Explain technical, economic, territorial, temporal, and bargaining comparability; otherwise state that the rate is not quantifiable from the available record.

2. WILLFULNESS ENHANCEMENT RISK — CRITICAL
   - Receipt of a report identifying a specific patent may be evidence of knowledge, but knowledge and continued activity alone do not establish willfulness
   - Assess case-specific evidence that the accused infringer knew of the patent and intentionally infringed; do not convert an FTO report, a HIGH risk rating, or the absence of a design-around into an automatic willfulness conclusion
   - Distinguish the jury's willfulness finding from the court's separate, discretionary enhanced-damages inquiry under Halo Electronics v. Pulse Electronics, 579 U.S. 93 (2016) and 35 U.S.C. § 284
   - Under 35 U.S.C. § 298, do not infer willfulness from failure to obtain or present advice of counsel
   - For each HIGH-risk patent, identify the evidence present, evidence missing, and counsel-review action; use calibrated language rather than a categorical exposure statement

3. LOST PROFITS vs REASONABLE ROYALTY (Rite-Hite Corp. v. Kelley, Inc., 56 F.3d 1538 (Fed. Cir. 1995))
   - Lost profits require case-specific but-for causation and proof, commonly assessed through Panduit or another accepted method. Practicing status or competition is relevant but does not make the remedy automatic, elective, or equal to an entire profit margin.
   - Under 35 U.S.C. § 284, a reasonable royalty is the statutory damages floor, not an NPE ceiling. Do not determine the remedy from entity type alone; identify proof of demand, acceptable noninfringing substitutes, capacity, sales causation, apportionment, and profit.
   - If patent holder has an established licensing program: reference the terms and territorial scope
   - If patent holder is an originator pharmaceutical company: note Panduit test factors (demand, absence of substitutes, manufacturing capability, quantifiable profit)

4. VOLUNTARY AND COMPULSORY LICENSING
   - If the patent holder has voluntary licensing programs (e.g., Gilead/MPP for HCV, AbbVie biosimilar settlements), note the territorial scope, royalty terms, and eligibility criteria
   - For essential medicines or global health compounds, note TRIPS flexibilities: compulsory licensing under TRIPS Article 31 (national emergency, public non-commercial use), Doha Declaration flexibilities, and TRIPS waiver provisions
   - These alternatives may reduce damages exposure or provide a non-litigation path to market

**For LOW risk:** One sentence noting residual exposure and basis for low assessment.
**For CLEAR risk:** State no prospective exposure only for the verified future acts and claims in scope. Preserve any unresolved past-act, jurisdiction, status, or damages-period question; do not say damages are nil merely from a current CLEAR label.

PART B: INJUNCTION RISK ASSESSMENT
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

**For HIGH and MEDIUM risk (full analysis):**

Apply the eBay Inc. v. MercExchange, LLC, 547 U.S. 388 (2006) four-factor test:

1. IRREPARABLE INJURY — Does the patent holder suffer harm money cannot fix?
   - Competition, market share, price erosion, licensing practice, causal nexus, delay, and other record evidence may be relevant. Do not assign the factor from competitor or NPE status alone.

2. INADEQUACY OF MONETARY DAMAGES — Can money fully compensate?
   - Evaluate the actual licensing record, calculability, causal harm, substitutes, and market evidence. A licence, unique product, or revenue threshold is evidence to weigh, not a categorical outcome.

3. BALANCE OF HARDSHIPS — Who is hurt more by the injunction?
   - Established patient population on generic → hardship to patients and public health
   - Early-stage product with no patient reliance → less hardship from delay
   - Multiple generic competitors already in market → less marginal harm from one more

4. PUBLIC INTEREST — Does an injunction serve the public?
   - Assess the case-specific health, access, safety, competition, and patent-policy evidence. Drug category, WHO listing, or FDA approval alone does not decide the factor.

PERMANENT INJUNCTION: Apply all four eBay factors without categorical rules based on entity type or industry.
PRELIMINARY INJUNCTION: Separately assess likelihood of success on the merits, likely irreparable harm absent preliminary relief, balance of equities, and public interest. Verify claim construction, infringement, validity, status, and timing evidence; do not infer availability from a HIGH label or enforcement history.

PHARMACEUTICAL-SPECIFIC considerations:
- Hatch-Waxman 30-month stay (small molecules): generally follows only when the NDA holder or patent owner brings a qualifying infringement action within 45 days after receiving Paragraph IV notice. Verify the applicable patent, notice, complaint, statutory timing, patent expiry, and any court order; do not describe the certification itself as an automatic stay or as an injunction.
- BPCIA patent dance (biologics): different timeline and preliminary injunction standards
- 180-day exclusivity for first ANDA filer: affects damages calculation window

**For LOW risk:** One sentence on residual injunction risk.
**For CLEAR risk:** One sentence: "Injunction risk is negligible given [expiry/invalidity/non-infringement basis]."
